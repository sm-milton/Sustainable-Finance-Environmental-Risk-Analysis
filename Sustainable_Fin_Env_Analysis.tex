\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

\subsection{Business context}\label{business-context}

I am an entry-level analyst at an investment firm that finances
\textbf{sustainable infrastructure} and applies
\textbf{responsible-investment} principles. My line manager asked for
four pieces of work:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Most common climate hazards affecting communities globally
\item
  Number of planned projects and total funding sought, by region
\item
  Ranked list of collaboration / investment opportunities
\item
  Best model to predict environmental controversy involvement, with
  evaluation
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{How I will work.} For every step I first explain \emph{why} I am
doing it and \emph{how}, then run the R code, then \emph{interpret} the
result. All data preparation is done in R (nothing is edited in Excel),
and I never type in data values by hand; every number comes from the two
CSV files.

The program is designed to generate revenue over more than four decades that supports preservation and stewardship, delivering co benefits in public health, air quality, stormwater management, and energy savings. It aligns with broader City goals of enhancing equitable greenspace access within walking distance for all residents, advancing greenhouse gas reduction efforts, and strengthening resilience through nature based strategies. Importantly, this carbon credit model is structured to continue scaling across all future forested property acquisitions the City makes, encouraging ongoing preservation while also generating additional revenue that can be reinvested to compound environmental and community impact over time.$\backslash$n$\backslash$nThe City is actively seeking partners to purchase these premium Carbon Plus credits and help spread the word about their availability.
